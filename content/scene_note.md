## Scene note

This note was written by a person from the STAC record and from looking at the quicklook. It is the only report this demo may treat as source text. No detector labelled this frame.

### Collect

`ICEYE_EYCKP5_20251024T135309Z_6724574_X52_SLF`. ICEYE-X52, spotlight, X-band, VV, descending, right-looking. The acquisition runs from 24 October 2025, 13:53:09 UTC to 13:53:24 UTC, about fourteen seconds. Incidence angle is about 32 degrees.

The footprint is roughly longitude -9.214 to -9.140 and latitude 38.660 to 38.718. That covers the 25 de Abril bridge across the Tagus, with Lisbon on one bank and Almada on the other.

The quicklook on disk is an 8-bit raster, 4096 by 4096, with pixel spacing about 1.22 m in the quicklook sidecar. The SLC (about 11 GB) and the GRD (about 484 MB) are not in this repository. The dwell video is the same collect, not a second product.

### What a person can see

The river is dark. A bright line crosses it where the bridge deck stands. Two compact brighter returns sit on that line, where the towers of the 25 de Abril stand. One bank is a dense field of bright returns from port structures and city blocks. The other bank is built ground rising toward hills. A bright smear lies in the water beside the deck. That smear is not named here.

These are readings of brightness and of a place the footprint is known to cover. They are not measurements of length, count, speed, or material.

### What this frame cannot support

- It is one pass. It cannot say what moved since another day.
- It has no AIS, no optical image, and no second sensor. A bright return on the water is not a vessel identification.
- Nothing in this repository has drawn a box, counted an object, or matched a detection to a track.
- Pixel brightness is a radar measurement, not a photograph. This note does not claim what the metal, the water, or the crews were doing.
- Questions about this scene may be answered only from the STAC fields and from this note. If those texts do not contain the answer, the answer is that the texts do not say.
